## 1. What the Benchmarking Module Does

The Benchmarking tab answers one question: **how does this organisation compare to the market?**

It takes the active dataset (the employee census loaded in Upload & Prepare), runs a battery of SQL aggregations against it, and compares each computed metric to a set of P25/median/P75 values from a chosen benchmark pack. The result is a full variance grid, a ranked set of quantified opportunities, and an optional AI narrative that interprets those numbers.

The module has two independent layers:

| Layer | What it does | Uses AI? |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Deterministic engine** | Computes all client metrics, runs the comparison, quantifies gaps, ranks opportunities | No |
| **AI report** | Interprets the deterministic numbers and writes a structured narrative | Yes — but only reads the deterministic output, never invents figures |

## 2. Built-in Benchmark Packs

There are **8 built-in packs**, all dated 2026, denominated in USD, and global in scope. They are stored as JSON files on the server and re-seeded on every restart — you cannot permanently edit them. To edit values, use **Duplicate** to create an editable project-owned copy.

All packs cover the same set of 8 functions. Sub-function shares (Finance, HR, IT, Procurement) are shared across all packs via a common library and inherited unless a pack explicitly overrides them.

### Financial Services
**Use when:** Banking, insurance, asset management.

**Key differences from Cross-Industry:**
- IT is **4–5× larger** (technology is the delivery channel, not just support): median 13% vs 3% of total FTE
- Compliance & Legal is **6–7× larger** due to regulatory burden: median 4% vs 0.6%
- Spans are tighter (median 5.5 vs 6.0) reflecting heavier supervisory requirements
- Layers are deeper (up to 9 vs 8)

---

### Technology & Software
**Use when:** Product-led software and technology businesses.

**Key differences from Cross-Industry:**
- R&D is the **dominant function** — median 32% of FTE vs 6% cross-industry
- Support function ratios (HR, Finance) expressed as % of total FTE run higher because there is little "Procurement" operational base to dilute them
- Structures are flatter: median 6 layers vs 7, higher IC:Mgr ratio (7.3 vs 6.1)
- IT internal function is smaller (median 5% vs 3%) because engineers sit in R&D

### Manufacturing & Industrials
**Use when:** Discrete manufacturing, industrial goods, supply chain heavy.

**Key differences:**
- Procurement carries the largest workforce (operational base)
- Spans in Procurement are very wide (median 9–12)
- Finance and HR are lean relative to total headcount

### Healthcare & Life Sciences
**Use when:** Pharma, hospitals, medical devices.

**Key differences:**
- R&D large (clinical and research headcount)
- Compliance & Legal elevated (regulatory affairs, quality)
- Deeper hierarchies due to clinical governance structures

### Retail & CPG
**Use when:** Consumer goods, retail, FMCG.

**Key differences:**
- Customer Support is large (store/service staff if included)
- Sales & Marketing carries significant share
- High proportion of HC below layer 5 (store/distribution staff)

---

### Energy & Utilities
**Use when:** Regulated utilities, oil & gas, renewables.

**Key differences:**
- Procurement large (operational / field workforce)
- IT growing due to digital transformation
- Compliance elevated (environmental and safety regulation)

---

### Professional Services
**Use when:** Consulting, law, accounting, advisory.

**Key differences:**
- R&D minimal
- Sales & Marketing moderate
- Higher manager ratios (client engagement model creates many supervisory layers)

## 3. Complete Metric Reference

There are **20 distinct metric definitions** across three scopes. A pack can carry any subset of them.

### Org-level metrics (apply to the whole organisation)

| Metric Key | Label | Unit | Direction | What it measures |
|------------|-------|------|-----------|-----------------|
| `avg_span_of_control` | Average span of control | Ratio | **Band** | Average direct reports across all people managers |
| `total_layers` | Organisational layers | Count | **Lower better** | Depth from top role to deepest reporting line |
| `mgr_pct_of_headcount` | Managers as % of headcount | % | **Lower better** | Share of the population with at least one direct report |
| `mgr_cost_pct_of_total_cost` | Management cost as % of total cost | % | **Lower better** | Share of total workforce cost carried by people managers |
| `ic_to_mgr_ratio` | Individual contributors per manager | Ratio | **Higher better** | ICs divided by managers |
| `pct_managers_below_target_span` | Managers below target span | % | **Lower better** | Share of managers below the configured target span (default 6) |
| `pct_single_report_managers` | Single-report managers | % | **Lower better** | Managers with exactly one direct report — clearest delayering signal |
| `pct_headcount_below_layer_5` | Headcount below layer 5 | % | **Higher better** | Share sitting at layer 5 or deeper — high = flat delivery base |
| `pct_fte_in_low_cost_locations` | FTE in low-cost locations | % | **Higher better** | Share of FTE in low-cost delivery centres |
| `revenue_per_fte` *(needs revenue)* | Revenue per FTE | Currency | **Higher better** | Company revenue ÷ total FTE — headline productivity |
| `fte_per_1m_revenue` *(needs revenue)* | FTE per $1M revenue | Ratio | **Lower better** | FTE required per million of revenue |

### Function-level metrics (computed per function)

| Metric Key | Label | Unit | Direction | What it measures |
|------------|-------|------|-----------|-----------------|
| `func_fte_pct_of_total_fte` | Function FTE as % of total FTE | % | **Lower better** | Function weight relative to the whole org |
| `func_fte_per_1000_employees` | Function FTE per 1,000 employees | per 1,000 | **Lower better** | Standard support-function staffing ratio |
| `func_cost_pct_of_total_cost` | Function cost as % of total cost | % | **Lower better** | Function cost weight vs total workforce cost |
| `func_avg_span` | Function average span | Ratio | **Band** | Avg direct reports per manager within the function |
| `func_layers` | Function layers | Count | **Lower better** | Distinct hierarchy levels within the function |
| `mgr_pct_of_headcount` | Managers as % of headcount | % | **Lower better** | Manager ratio within the function |
| `func_cost_per_fte` *(needs currency)* | Function cost per FTE | Currency | **Lower better** | Fully loaded cost per FTE in the function |
| `func_cost_pct_of_revenue` *(needs revenue)* | Function cost as % of revenue | % | **Lower better** | Function workforce cost vs company revenue |
| `fte_per_1m_revenue` *(needs revenue)* | FTE per $1M revenue | Ratio | **Lower better** | Function FTE per million of revenue |

### Sub-function-level metrics

| Metric Key | Label | Unit | Direction | What it measures |
|------------|-------|------|-----------|-----------------|
| `subfunc_fte_pct_of_function_fte` | Sub-function FTE as % of function FTE | % | **Lower better** | Sub-function weight **inside its parent function** |
| `subfunc_cost_pct_of_function_cost` | Sub-function cost as % of function cost | % | **Lower better** | Sub-function cost share inside its parent |
| `subfunc_avg_span` | Sub-function average span | Ratio | **Band** | Avg direct reports per manager in the sub-function |

### Direction definitions

| Direction | "Favourable" means | "Unfavourable" means |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Lower better** | Client value < P25 | Client value > P75 |
| **Higher better** | Client value > P75 | Client value < P25 |
| **Band** | Client value within P25–P75 | Either extreme (too low OR too high) |

If no P25/P75 are provided for a metric, the system falls back to a ±10% corridor around the median.

### Context requirements

These metrics are **suppressed** (shown as "withheld") until the relevant context is set in the sidebar:

| Context needed | Which metrics unlock |
|----------------|---------------------|
| **Reporting currency** (+ FX rate if different from pack) | `func_cost_per_fte` |
| **Annual revenue** | `revenue_per_fte`, `fte_per_1m_revenue`, `func_cost_pct_of_revenue` |

## 4. How to Upload a Custom Pack

### When to use a custom pack
- You have a client-specific peer set with proprietary P25/median/P75 data
- The built-in packs don't match the client's sector
- You want to benchmark against a specific geographic or size band

### Step-by-step

**Step 1 — Download the template**
Click **Download template** in the left sidebar. If a pack is already active, the template pre-fills with that pack's current values as a starting point.

**Step 2 — Fill in the Pack Info sheet**

| Field | Required? | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| Pack name | **Yes** | Shown in the pack picker |
| Industry | Optional | e.g. Financial Services |
| Region | Optional | e.g. Global, EMEA |
| Size band | Optional | e.g. 1,000–10,000 |
| Currency | **Yes** | ISO code for any cost values (e.g. USD, GBP) |
| Effective year | Optional | Year the data represents |
| Source | Optional | Where the data came from — recorded for audit |
| Notes | Optional | Caveats, peer set composition, exclusions |

**Step 3 — Fill in Functional Benchmarks sheet**

One row per function. Use function names from the **Reference Lists** sheet — these are the A&M master taxonomy names that OrgSight rationalises client data onto, so the comparison join is exact.

Each metric column accepts a **median** value. The matching `(P25)` and `(P75)` columns are optional but strongly recommended — without them, the system uses a ±10% band around the median, which is less precise.

Available metric columns:
- FTE % of Total FTE
- FTE per 1000 Employees
- Cost % of Total Cost
- Cost per FTE
- Cost % of Revenue
- Avg Span
- Layers
- Manager %

**Step 4 — Optionally fill Subfunction Benchmarks**

One row per function + subfunction pair. Metrics: FTE % of Function FTE, Cost % of Function Cost, Avg Span.

**Step 5 — Optionally fill Structural Benchmarks**

Organisation-wide structural metrics (span, layers, manager ratios). These override what would otherwise be compared against the org-level benchmarks.

**Step 6 — Upload**

Click **Upload pack** in the sidebar. The system:
1. Validates the workbook and reports row-level warnings (e.g. P25 > P75 auto-swap)
2. Creates a new custom pack owned by this project
3. Auto-selects it and triggers an immediate preview recompute

**Parser behaviour:**
- If median is blank but P25 and P75 both exist, median = midpoint
- If P25 > P75, they are swapped and a warning is shown
- Any metric column left entirely blank is simply ignored
- At least one metric must have a value or the upload is rejected

### Managing custom packs

| Action | Works on |
|--------|---------|
| **Duplicate** | Any pack (built-in or custom) → creates an editable copy |
| **Delete** | Custom packs only; built-in packs cannot be deleted |
| **Edit values** | Custom packs only (duplicated packs are editable) |

## 5. How Calculations Work Against Your Data

Every number in the Benchmarking tab is computed by running **SQL directly against your loaded census** (the `employees` DuckDB table). Nothing is estimated or approximated.

### Step 1 — Column resolution

The engine first figures out which physical columns in your dataset map to each analytical concept:

| Concept | Preferred column | Fallback |
|---------|-----------------|---------|
| Function | `Rationalised Function` *(from Rationalise module)* | Dataset-mapped function column |
| Sub-function | `Rationalised Subfunction` | Dataset-mapped sub-function column |
| FTE | Dataset-mapped FTE column | 1.0 per head |
| Cost | Dataset-mapped FLC/cost column | 0 (cost metrics suppressed) |
| Country | Dataset-mapped country column | (low-cost location metric unavailable) |
| Level | Computed `Level` column | (structural metrics unavailable) |
| Span | Computed `Span` column | (span metrics unavailable) |

**If you've run the Rationalise module**, function names have been normalised onto the A&M master taxonomy — the same vocabulary the benchmark packs use. This gives exact joins and maximises coverage.

### Step 2 — SQL aggregation

A single battery of SQL runs against the `employees` table, computing:

```sql
-- Per scope (org / function / sub-function):
COUNT(*)                                    → headcount
SUM(FTE)                                    → total_fte
SUM(Cost)                                   → total_cost
COUNT WHERE Span > 0                        → managers
COUNT WHERE Span <= 0                       → individual contributors (ICs)
SUM(Span) WHERE Span > 0                   → total subordinates
SUM(Cost) WHERE Span > 0                   → manager_cost
COUNT WHERE Span = 1                        → single_report_managers
COUNT WHERE Span > 0 AND Span < target_span → managers_below_target
MAX(Level)                                  → max_level (deepest layer)
COUNT DISTINCT Level                        → level_depth
COUNT WHERE Level >= 5                      → headcount_below_L5
SUM(FTE) WHERE Country IN (low_cost list)   → low_cost_fte
```

This runs three times: once for the whole org, once grouped by function, once grouped by function+subfunction.

### Step 3 — Metric derivation

Each registry metric is derived from the aggregates using a formula. Examples:

| Metric | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| Avg span of control | `subordinates / managers` |
| Manager % | `(managers / headcount) × 100` |
| Function FTE share | `(function_fte / org_fte) × 100` |
| Function FTE per 1,000 | `(function_fte / org_headcount) × 1,000` |
| Single-report managers % | `(single_report_mgrs / managers) × 100` |
| Managers below target % | `(mgrs_below_target / managers) × 100` |
| Cost per FTE | `total_cost / total_fte × fx_rate` |
| IC:Mgr ratio | `ics / managers` |

### Step 4 — Function mapping

Client function names are matched to benchmark pack function names in order:
1. **Manual override** — if you've set a mapping in the benchmark configuration, that wins
2. **Case-insensitive exact match** — `"R&D"` matches `"R&D"`, `"r&d"` also matches
3. **Unmapped** — anything else is excluded from function-level benchmarking (but still counted in org totals)

Multiple client functions can be mapped to one benchmark function — their aggregates are summed before comparison.

Coverage = (FTE in mapped functions) / (total org FTE). Anything below 60% triggers a warning.

### Step 5 — Comparison

For each metric where both a client value and a benchmark value exist:

```
delta     = client_value − median
delta_pct = (client_value − median) / |median| × 100
position  = below_p25 | in_band | above_p75
verdict   = favourable | in_line | unfavourable
severity  = |delta_pct|
```

### Step 6 — Gap quantification

Unfavourable variances are translated into FTE and cost exposure. The gap formulas by metric:

| Metric | FTE gap | Cost gap |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Function FTE % of total | `(delta% / 100) × org_total_fte` | — |
| Function FTE per 1,000 | `(delta / 1,000) × org_headcount` | — |
| Function cost % of total | — | `(delta% / 100) × org_total_cost` |
| Function cost per FTE | — | `delta × function_fte` |
| Avg span (below median) | `managers − (subordinates / benchmark_span)` | `excess_managers × avg_mgr_cost` |
| Manager % of headcount | `(delta% / 100) × headcount` | `excess_managers × avg_mgr_cost` |
| Single-report managers % | `(delta% / 100) × managers` | `excess_managers × avg_mgr_cost` |
| Sub-function FTE share | `(delta% / 100) × parent_function_fte` | — |

**Savings range** = `cost_gap × [realization_low, realization_high]`

Default realization: 60–70%. Adjustable in the Assumptions section of the sidebar.

### Step 7 — Opportunity ranking

One opportunity card is created per group (function or org-level), using a single primary metric so the same headcount isn't counted three times. Priority order for functional opportunities: cost share → FTE share → FTE per 1,000. Opportunities are sorted by savings_high descending.

**Buckets:**
- **Functional** — oversized functions (FTE share, cost share, cost per FTE)
- **Structural** — management layers, spans, single-report managers

These two buckets draw on the same workforce and **must never be added together**.

**Confidence levels:**
| Level | Condition |
|-------|-----------|
| **Low** | Coverage < 60% |
| **Medium** | No P25/P75 available, or delta% < 15% |
| **High** | Full P25/P75 band, delta% ≥ 15%, coverage ≥ 60% |

### Health score

`health_score = (in_line + favourable metrics) / total_measured_metrics × 100`

| Score | Band |
|-------|------|
| 75–100 | Strong |
| 55–74 | Mixed |
| 35–54 | Stretched |
| 0–34 | Critical |

## 6. Does It Update When Your Data Changes?

**Yes — automatically and immediately.**

| What changes | What triggers |
|-------------|--------------|
| Switch baseline ↔ scenario in the dataset selector | New dataset/scenario IDs sent to the preview endpoint → full recompute |
| Switch to a different dataset entirely | Same as above |
| Change any sidebar control (pack, assumptions, context) | 450ms debounce → fresh `POST /benchmark/preview` |
| Save benchmark configuration | Config persisted to DB; used as default on next load |
| Re-upload or re-process data in Upload & Prepare | DuckDB table is replaced; next preview fetch reads the new table |
| Run Rationalise | Adds/updates `Rationalised Function` column; next preview uses cleaner function matching |
| Generate a new AI report | Always runs `build_comparison` fresh; narrative written from that fresh output |

**Saved AI reports are snapshots.** A saved report captures the deterministic output at the moment it was generated. If you upload new data or change the pack afterwards, you need to **Regenerate** the report to see updated numbers. The saved report clearly shows the dataset name and date to help identify stale reports.

The preview grid (the four tabs below the KPI tiles) is always live and always reflects the current dataset + current config.

## 7. The Four Analysis Tabs

### Overview tab

Three charts that give a visual summary:

**1. Variance against benchmark median** (diverging bar chart)
- Shows the top 16 metrics, sorted by severity
- Bars pointing right = client is above median (red = unfavourable where higher is worse)
- Bars pointing left = client is below median (red = unfavourable where lower is worse)
- Coloured by verdict: red = unfavourable, green = favourable, grey = in-line

**2. Function size vs efficiency** (bubble/quadrant chart)
- X axis = function size (FTE)
- Y axis = efficiency variance (distance from median)
- Bubble area = workforce cost
- Top-right quadrant = large and expensive (highest priority)

**3. Where the value sits** (savings waterfall)
- Cumulative savings build-up, largest opportunity first
- Shows upper-bound savings only (realization_high)
- Up to 8 opportunities shown

---

### Opportunities tab

Cards for each ranked opportunity, filterable by All / Functional / Structural.

Each card shows:
- Function/sub-function name and the primary metric driving the opportunity
- Client value vs benchmark median
- % variance and position in the band
- FTE gap equivalent
- Savings range (e.g. $X.Xm–$X.Xm)
- Confidence level: Low / Medium / High

The tab header displays a count (e.g. "Opportunities (10)") — this is the total number of quantified opportunities across both buckets.

Note at the bottom: "Functional and structural sizing overlap — do not add them together."

---

### All Variances tab

A full sortable table of every measured metric. Columns:

| Column | What it shows |
|--------|--------------|
| Scope | org / function / subfunction |
| Area | Function or sub-function name |
| Metric | Metric label |
| Client | Client's computed value |
| P25 | Benchmark 25th percentile |
| Median | Benchmark 50th percentile |
| P75 | Benchmark 75th percentile |
| Variance % | `(client − median) / median × 100` |
| Position | Above / In band / Below |

Clicking column headers sorts the table. This is where you can inspect every individual data point that feeds the summary tiles.

---

### Function Mapping tab

Two panels:

**Matched** — client function → benchmark function pairs. Shows which client function names were successfully matched and how (exact match or manual override).

**Unmapped** — function names that couldn't be matched. These are excluded from function-level benchmarking and dilute coverage. Amber dots mark each unmapped entry.

The note at the bottom directs you to run the **Rationalise** module, which is the single biggest lever on coverage because it normalises all client function names onto the A&M master taxonomy.

## 8. The AI Report — What It Does and Doesn't Do

### What it does

The AI report (`Generate AI report`) runs a six-section structured narrative over the deterministic comparison. It is written by an LLM that receives the full output of `build_comparison` as a pinned fact block — every number it quotes comes directly from that output.

For analytical sections it may also run up to **4 read-only SQL queries** against your employee census to investigate specific variances (e.g. "which sub-units drive the R&D span problem?"). These queries and their results are shown in collapsible **"drill-down queries ran"** panels below each section.

### What it cannot do

- **Invent numbers.** The system prompt explicitly instructs the model that every figure must come from the FACTS block or a query result. Savings ranges are always stated with the realization assumption.
- **Run write queries.** Only `SELECT` statements are permitted; no DDL or DML.
- **Change the deterministic output.** The AI layer is purely a narrator — the variance grid, opportunity cards and charts are always identical to the deterministic preview regardless of what the AI says.

### Streaming behaviour

The report streams in real time over Server-Sent Events:
1. `deterministic` event arrives first → charts and scorecard render immediately
2. For each section: optionally a drilldown phase, then tokens stream in progressively
3. `done` event arrives → full assembled report is available to save or export

## 9. The Six AI Report Sections

### 1. Executive Summary *(The Headline)*
- A single verdict sentence on how the organisation compares
- Three most important findings, each with client value vs benchmark side by side
- Total opportunity as a range with realization assumption stated
- Explicit note that functional and structural opportunity overlap
- Target: under 300 words — the only section some readers will see

*Visuals: none (uses the hero scorecard above)*

---

### 2. Function-by-Function Variance *(Where the Gaps Are)*
- Works through functions with the largest unfavourable variances, most significant first
- For each: the variance against benchmark, FTE and cost translation, what inside the function drives it (from drilldown evidence where available)
- Functions at or better than benchmark are noted briefly
- Small functions are grouped rather than listed individually

*Visuals: variance diverging chart, function quadrant*
*Uses drilldown SQL: yes (investigates which sub-units, grades or locations drive each variance)*

---

### 3. Structure, Spans and Layers *(How the Org Is Built)*
- Analyses span of control, layer count, management density and single-report managers
- Explains how these interact — low average span + deep layering is a different problem from wide spans in a flat structure
- Identifies which parts of the org drive structural variance
- Flags where a structural variance may be a legitimate feature of the operating model rather than an inefficiency

*Visuals: span distribution chart, layer pyramid*
*Uses drilldown SQL: yes*

---

### 4. Root Causes and Cross-Cutting Patterns *(Why It Looks Like This)*
- Steps back from individual functions to identify 2–4 patterns explaining multiple variances at once
- Examples: duplicated capability across BUs, high-cost location footprint, grade structure that has drifted senior
- Each pattern is labelled as a hypothesis with the evidence that supports it and what would confirm or disprove it
- Clearly distinguishes what the data shows from what it merely suggests

*Visuals: cost vs span scatter*
*Uses drilldown SQL: yes*

---

### 5. Quantified Opportunity *(What It Is Worth)*
- Walks through ranked opportunities
- For each material one: size as a range, confidence level and why, practical route to capture, key risks
- Candid about which opportunities are clean and which depend on untested assumptions
- Restates once that functional and structural sizing overlap
- Does not produce a grand total that adds the two buckets

*Visuals: savings waterfall, opportunity cards*
*Uses drilldown SQL: no*

---

### 6. Recommended Actions *(What to Do Next)*
- Sequenced plan across three horizons:
  - **First 30 days** — validate and mobilise (confirm assumptions, scope the work)
  - **60 days** — design the target state (operating model, org design decisions)
  - **90 days+** — execute and track (implementation, change management, measurement)
- Each action has an owner archetype, the specific question it resolves, and the opportunity it unlocks
- Ends with data gaps that would sharpen the analysis if closed

*Visuals: roadmap timeline*
*Uses drilldown SQL: no*

## 10. Limitations and Guardrails

### Always check these before client use

| Issue | Indicator | Resolution |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Low function coverage | Coverage meter < 60% / amber warning | Run Rationalise, then re-map unmapped functions in benchmark config |
| Currency mismatch | "Cost metrics suppressed" warning | Set Reporting currency in Client Context; add FX rate if needed |
| Revenue metrics unavailable | Listed under "Metrics deliberately withheld" | Set Annual revenue in Client Context |
| Stale saved report | Report title shows an old date | Regenerate from current data |
| Built-in pack mismatch | Cross-industry used for a specific sector | Switch to the sector pack or upload a client-specific pack |
| No cost column mapped | All cost benchmarks unavailable | Map the FLC/cost column in Upload & Prepare |

### The overlap rule
