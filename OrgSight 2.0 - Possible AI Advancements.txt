1. AI-Powered Org Design Recommender ("What-If Copilot")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pain today:
  Consultants manually drag-drop employees, flag positions, add new roles, and
  iterate through scenarios one node at a time. For a 5,000-person org, designing
  even one scenario is hours of tedious work.

Idea:
  An AI assistant that takes a natural language directive and proposes a batch of
  structural changes as a draft scenario.

  Examples:
  - "Consolidate all HR Business Partners under a single VP, eliminate the
     regional HR Director layer"
  - "Reduce the Finance function by 15% FTE, prioritizing roles below Grade 7
     in shared services locations"
  - "Flatten the Technology org to max 6 layers, target average span of 7"

  The AI would analyze the current baseline, generate a proposed set of
  moves/flags/adds with change reasons, and present them as a reviewable diff
  before applying. The consultant stays in control — they review, tweak,
  accept/reject individual changes — but the initial heavy lifting is automated.

Why it matters:
  This is the single biggest time sink in the platform. Converting a strategic
  directive into 50-200 individual org chart mutations is where consultants
  spend most of their project time.

AI Type: LLM + graph reasoning
Effort: High


2. Intelligent Data Quality & Cleanup Agent
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pain today:
  Users upload messy Excel census data, run validation, then manually review and
  fix issues — duplicate IDs, missing managers, circular references, inconsistent
  titles, misspelled countries, mixed date formats. The current validation is
  rule-based and binary (flag or don't flag).

Idea:
  An AI agent that goes beyond flagging to auto-resolving data quality issues
  with confidence scores:

  - Fuzzy manager matching — when Line Manager ID doesn't match exactly, suggest
    the closest match by name/title similarity ("Did you mean John Smith
    (ID: 4521) instead of non-existent ID 4520?")
  - Title standardization — cluster messy job titles into canonical forms
    ("Sr. Analyst", "Senior Analyst", "Snr Analyst" → "Senior Analyst").
    The existing CENSUS_RATIONALISATION tool does this separately — integrate
    it directly.
  - Missing data imputation — predict likely FTE (usually 1.0), Grade, or
    Country based on role/entity/manager patterns in the same dataset.
  - Anomaly flagging — "This Director has 0 direct reports and an FLC of $450K
    — outlier compared to peer Directors averaging 8 reports and $280K FLC"

Why it matters:
  Data prep currently consumes 30-40% of project time. Reducing it to a
  review-and-approve workflow would be transformative.

AI Type: NLP + fuzzy matching + anomaly detection
Effort: Medium


3. Smart Scenario Comparison & Narrative Generation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pain today:
  After building 2-3 scenarios, consultants manually compare summary metrics,
  dimensional breakdowns, and change logs, then write up a narrative for the
  client deck explaining trade-offs.

Idea:
  AI-generated scenario comparison narratives and executive briefings:

  - Auto-generate a structured comparison: "Scenario B removes 12% more
    headcount than A but concentrates reductions in India (78% of removals),
    creating geographic concentration risk. Scenario A spreads reductions more
    evenly but preserves 3 thin management layers that B eliminates."
  - Generate risk callouts: "Scenario C promotes 4 individual contributors to
    manager roles, creating 4 new 1:1 reporting lines — contradicts the stated
    goal of increasing average span"
  - Produce a draft PowerPoint narrative slide alongside the existing PPT export
    — not just charts, but the story

Why it matters:
  The deliverable isn't just the org chart — it's the story. Automating the
  "so what?" interpretation turns OrgSight from an analysis tool into a
  recommendation engine.

AI Type: LLM generation
Effort: Medium

4. Predictive Attrition & Flight Risk Overlay
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea:
  If census data includes tenure, performance ratings, or compensation bands,
  build a flight risk model that overlays on the org chart:

  - Color-code nodes by predicted attrition probability
  - Warn when a scenario flags a role where the remaining team has high flight
    risk ("Removing this manager leaves 6 reports under a skip-level manager —
    3 of 6 are tenure <1 year, historically a 40% attrition segment")
  - Factor attrition into savings projections — "Your scenario saves $2.1M
    gross, but with expected backfill of 15% of remaining roles, net savings
    is $1.7M"

Why it matters:
  Org design in a vacuum ignores the human reality. This bridges the gap between
  structural optimization and workforce stability.

AI Type: Predictive ML
Effort: Medium-High


5. AI-Driven Spans & Layers Optimization Engine
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pain today:
  Spans & Layers analysis identifies problems (1:1 managers, thin layers,
  below-target spans) but the consultant must manually figure out how to fix them.

Idea:
  An optimization engine that proposes specific structural changes to meet
  target metrics:

  - Input: "Target average span of 7, max 6 layers, eliminate all 1:1
    reporting lines"
  - Output: A ranked list of proposed changes — "Remove Layer 4 in Marketing
    (merge 12 managers up), consolidate 3 Finance teams into 2, promote 4 team
    leads to absorb direct reports" — with projected before/after metrics
  - Constraint-aware: respect geographic requirements ("each country must retain
    a local HR lead"), grade bands, regulatory needs
  - Visualize the impact as a before/after butterfly chart showing how the
    pyramid shape changes

Why it matters:
  This turns the descriptive "here's what's wrong" into prescriptive "here's
  how to fix it" — the actual consulting value-add.

AI Type: Constraint optimization + LLM
Effort: High


6. Natural Language Query Interface ("Ask OrgSight")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea:
  A conversational interface layered over the dataset:

  - "How many managers in APAC have fewer than 3 direct reports?"
  - "What's the average fully loaded cost for Senior Analysts in the UK vs
     Germany?"
  - "Show me everyone who reports up through Sarah Chen"
  - "Which department has the highest ratio of managers to ICs?"

  The AI translates natural language into filters, aggregations, and crosstabs
  against the loaded dataset — then renders the answer as a table, chart, or
  org chart view. Think of it as a domain-specific copilot that knows org census
  data semantics.

Why it matters:
  Not every question fits neatly into the predefined modules. Consultants often
  need ad-hoc slicing that currently requires Excel side-analysis.

AI Type: Text-to-query LLM
Effort: Medium


7. Activity Analysis Intelligence
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pain today:
  The Activity Analysis module requires consultants to manually map roles to
  activities with percentage allocations, then manually define savings levers.
  This is educated guesswork based on interviews and workshops.

Idea:
  - Auto-suggest activity allocations based on job title/role using industry
    benchmarks or LLM reasoning ("A typical HR Business Partner spends ~30% on
    employee relations, 25% on talent management...")
  - Lever recommendation engine — given the activity mix, suggest which
    activities are most automatable: "Data entry and report generation activities
    (currently 22% of Finance FTE) have high automation potential — similar
    organizations have achieved 60-80% reduction"
  - Benchmark comparison — "Your IT org spends 35% of time on incident
    management vs industry median of 22% — investigate triage process"

Why it matters:
  Activity analysis is the most assumption-heavy part of the platform.
  AI-informed defaults dramatically improve speed and defensibility of
  assumptions.

AI Type: LLM + benchmarks
Effort: Medium

8. Change Impact Simulation & Ripple Analysis
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea:
  When a consultant proposes a change (e.g., removing a manager), AI simulates
  the cascading organizational impact:

  - Skills gap analysis: "Removing this role eliminates the only Python
    expertise in the Analytics team"
  - Workload redistribution: "The 8 direct reports will be absorbed by the
    skip-level manager, increasing their span from 5 to 13 — above the
    10-person threshold"
  - Communication chain impact: "This increases the average chain length from
    CEO to IC in this division from 5.2 to 6.1 layers"
  - Cost ripple: "3 of the absorbed reports are likely candidates for grade
    uplift based on new responsibilities, adding ~$45K to the scenario cost"

AI Type: Graph analysis + LLM
Effort: Medium-High


9. Org Health Score & Benchmarking Dashboard
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea:
  An AI-computed composite org health score with dimensional breakdown:

  - Structural health: span distribution, layer count, 1:1 ratio, thin layer
    prevalence
  - Cost efficiency: FLC per IC, manager overhead ratio, cost concentration
    by geography
  - Hierarchy health: average chain length, orphan risk, single-point-of-failure
    managers
  - Diversity indicators: geographic/entity distribution, grade band equity

  Each dimension scored 0-100 with AI-generated commentary. Track the score
  across scenarios — "Scenario B improves structural health from 62 to 78 but
  reduces geographic diversity from 71 to 54."

  Stretch: benchmark against anonymized peer organizations or industry standards.

AI Type: Scoring model + LLM narrative
Effort: Medium


10. Intelligent Report & Deck Generator
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea:
  Go beyond data exports to AI-authored deliverable drafts:

  - Select a scenario → generate a complete findings deck with:
    - Executive summary slide with key metrics and recommendations
    - Current-state analysis (spans, layers, cost, structure)
    - Proposed future-state with rationale
    - Implementation timeline (from phasing data)
    - Risk assessment and mitigation
    - Appendix with detailed change log
  - Output as editable PowerPoint using the existing python-pptx infrastructure
  - The consultant edits and refines rather than starting from a blank deck

Why it matters:
  The final deliverable is always a deck. If OrgSight can draft 70% of it,
  that's days saved per engagement.

AI Type: LLM + template engine
Effort: Medium


11. Collaborative AI Advisor for Real-Time Modeling
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea:
  A persistent AI sidebar during org chart editing that proactively surfaces
  insights:

  - As the user drags a node: "Moving this team under Operations would create a
    3rd thin layer — consider also moving their peer team to avoid this"
  - After a batch of changes: "You've flagged 8 roles in India but only 2 in
    the UK for the same function — is the geographic distribution intentional?"
  - Detects patterns: "You're creating a new manager layer where the current
    span is only 4 — this contradicts the project's flatten-and-widen objective"
  - Suggests next actions: "Based on the changes so far, 3 remaining 1:1
    managers in this branch could be consolidated"

  This is the "copilot in the room" — always watching, never blocking, surfacing
  things the consultant might miss during intensive modeling sessions.

AI Type: LLM + rule engine
Effort: High
